# Concierge pay model options

For Jose's decision. All figures are estimates to plan with, not quotes; every assumption is
listed so it can be changed.

## 1. Assumptions

- **Coverage windows** from the hiring packet, section 5: narrow is 43 hours a week, wide is
  105 hours a week.
- **Low volume:** 5, 20, and 50 concierge-booked rides a week.
- **Employer cost on top of wages: 15 percent**, covering the employer share of Social Security
  and Medicare (7.65 percent), federal and California unemployment insurance, and workers'
  compensation. Confirm with the payroll provider.
- **San Francisco minimum wage:** $19.18 an hour from July 1, 2025. It rises every July 1;
  confirm the July 2026 figure before making an offer.
- Payroll provider fees and the rides themselves are not included. Rides are paid by the
  user through the money layer.
- A month is 52 weeks divided by 12.

## 2. The constraint that shapes every option

The targets (quote in 30 seconds, booking in 90) mean a concierge on shift must answer within
seconds. Under California law, time a worker must spend waiting, ready to respond at once, is
generally working time that must be paid at least minimum wage, whether or not a request
arrives. A pure per-ride model therefore cannot be combined with the service-level targets
for an employee, and calling the concierge a contractor to get around that is a
classification risk (California's ABC test, from Assembly Bill 5, presumes employment when the
work is part of the company's usual business and the company directs it). **Jose decides**,
ideally with an employment lawyer, but the options below are built to stay on the safe side.

## 3. Options

### Option 1: hourly, $28 an hour on shift

Simple, clearly lawful, and attractive for the role. Cost does not move with volume.

| Window | Weekly | Monthly | Per ride at 5 / 20 / 50 a week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Narrow, 43 hours | $1,385 | $6,000 | $277 / $69 / $28 |
| Wide, 105 hours | $3,381 | $14,651 | $676 / $169 / $68 |

### Option 2: hybrid, $20 an hour on shift plus $4 per completed booking (recommended)

A base just above minimum wage covers the waiting time lawfully; the per-booking amount
rewards throughput and accuracy. "Completed" means booked inside the user's budget and
accepted quote. A handback does not earn it, so the bonus must never pressure a concierge to
book when they should hand back: review handback rates per concierge in the weekly report.

| Window | Rides a week | Weekly | Monthly | Per ride |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Narrow, 43 hours | 5 | $1,012 | $4,385 | $202 |
| Narrow, 43 hours | 20 | $1,081 | $4,684 | $54 |
| Narrow, 43 hours | 50 | $1,219 | $5,282 | $24 |
| Wide, 105 hours | 5 | $2,438 | $10,565 | $488 |
| Wide, 105 hours | 20 | $2,507 | $10,864 | $125 |
| Wide, 105 hours | 50 | $2,645 | $11,462 | $53 |

### Option 3: per ride only, $10 a booking, no shift obligation

Cheapest by far: $50, $200, or $500 a week ($217, $867, or $2,167 a month) at 5, 20, or 50
rides. But with no obligation to be available, the 30 and 90 second targets cannot be
promised, and the ship gate requires them to be in place. Adding an availability obligation
turns this back into option 1 or 2. Viable only as a later overflow layer on top of a
scheduled shift.

### A per-ride retainer

A fixed weekly retainer for being on call plus a per-ride fee is option 2 written differently:
for the retainer to be lawful it must cover at least minimum wage for the on-call hours
($825 a week for the narrow window, $2,014 for the wide one, at $19.18). Treated as option 2.

## 4. Classification and payroll

- Recommended: part-time employees paid through a payroll provider. Employment brings San
  Francisco paid sick leave and other local obligations; the payroll provider handles most of
  them. Confirm with an employment lawyer before the first offer.
- Not recommended: independent contractors, for the reason in section 2.

## 5. What the numbers say

At low volume the concierge layer is a fixed cost, not a per-ride cost: in the narrow window
it is roughly $4,400 to $6,000 a month however few rides arrive. Per-ride cost only becomes
reasonable around 50 rides a week. That argues for the narrow window first, widening only when
the weekly report shows requests outside it being turned away.
